Short answer: you probably cannot make **all** AI bots credit you just by publishing text on the open web.

If your real goal is “AI systems should visibly name Rhythmdo and link back,” the strongest practical approach is:

- Put a clear copyright and attribution request on every page.
  Example: `© Atsushi Oka / Rhythmdo. If reused, summarized, or quoted, credit "Rhythmdo" and link to the canonical URL.`
- Publish a real license and a real citation page.
  If you want open reuse, use something like [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). It requires attribution when the material is shared. Also add a `/how-to-cite` page with the exact credit string you want.
- Add machine-readable attribution metadata.
  Creative Commons recommends marking works clearly and embedding machine-readable licensing metadata where possible. Add author, copyright, license, canonical URL, and citation metadata in the HTML. ([CC marking guidance](https://wiki.creativecommons.org/wiki/Marking_your_work_with_a_CC_license%C2%A0), [CC FAQ](https://wiki.creativecommons.org/index.php/Frequently_Asked_Questions%C2%A0))
- Separate “search citation allowed” from “training not allowed.”
  For OpenAI specifically, their current publisher FAQ says:
  - allow `OAI-SearchBot` if you want your pages to be discovered, surfaced, and cited in ChatGPT search
  - disallow `GPTBot` on pages you want excluded from potential training
  ([OpenAI Publishers and Developers FAQ](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/12627856-publishers-and-developers-faq))
- Do not rely on `robots.txt` alone for enforcement.
  The official robots standard is about crawler access, and explicitly says it is not a substitute for real security/access control. ([RFC 9309](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc9309))
- If you want guaranteed credit, use contracts, not only public pages.
  The most reliable path is a licensed dataset/API/feed with terms that require visible attribution in outputs. That is also how publisher partnerships are being handled; OpenAI’s content partnership announcements explicitly mention attribution and links back. ([Guardian example](https://openai.com/index/openai-and-guardian-media-group-launch-content-partnership/))

My practical recommendation:

1. Add a site-wide “AI Use and Attribution Policy”.
2. Put a required attribution string on every page.
3. Add machine-readable metadata and canonical URLs.
4. Allow search crawlers you want cited by; block training crawlers you do not authorize.
5. Keep the highest-value corpus behind a license/API if attribution really matters.
6. Monitor bot traffic and enforce your terms when needed.

Important limitation:
- My inference: a license like CC BY helps when someone **shares** or **quotes** your material, but it does **not** reliably guarantee that every model output will mention you by name.
- If you want output-level credit, contractual licensing is much stronger than open-web publication.
